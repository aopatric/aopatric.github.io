Hi there! I'm Angel, and I recently graduated from MIT (May 2026) with a B.S. in AI and Decision Making. I'm currently enrolled in the MEng program at MIT, and am using this Fall 2026 semester to work on independent research projects before returning to complete my thesis. Recently, I wrapped up a revisit of [my work](https://github.com/aopatric/post-training-EoS) on the Edge of Stability phenomenon for language model post-training, and at the moment I'm actively working on a [reward hacking project](https://github.com/aopatric/qwen-steering) on Qwen3-4B studying how we can use probing, activation patching, and steering methods to distinguish hacked solves from legitimate ones to inform active intervention methods for model alignment.

My time as an undergrad was spent across a few different research groups across MIT. I was fortunate enough to help design and prototype a benchmark for spatial reasoning in both neuro-symbolic and language-based models built around the inverse origami problem (later published by the lab as [OrigamiBench](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/68020)) with Prof. Armando Solar-Lezama's group at CSAIL, contribute core training and communication infrastructure to a decentralized and data-private solution for federated learning ([SONAR Web](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3737899.3768523), ACM FLEdge-AI '25, Best Presentation) with the Camera Culture group at the Media Lab, and adapt masked language modeling approaches to single-cell transcriptomics data from the CELLxGENE Census for imputation and classification with the Edelman Lab at IMES.

Outside of my MIT-affiliated work, my personal research interests lie heavily in the alignment of highly capable models, from the very first training step. Aside from my reward hacking study, I have much interest in the existing literature on sparse autoencoders and activation patching, as these methods seem most closely aligned with my end goal for the scalable, performant, capable, and most importantly safe models of the future. In that vein, I've also been building up intuition from the ground up in my [toy-transformers](https://github.com/aopatric/toy-transformers) repo, which contains notebooks exploring 0-, 1-, and 2-layer attention-only transformers in the style of [A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html) (Elhage et al., 2021). Feel free to poke around!

When I'm not working on one of the above things, I'm often doing any one of producing or DJing or consuming music, poking around with the latest AI coding tools, or just hanging out with my cat, Cillian. I'm also a fan of MMA and have been training BJJ for a little over a year now. I'm always open to dialogue and I deeply enjoy discussing research topics, so feel free to reach out!
